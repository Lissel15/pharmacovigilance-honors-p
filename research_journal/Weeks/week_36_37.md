# Research Journal: Weeks 36 to 37 (Stanford NLP, Random Forest & Vocabulary Extraction)
**Project:** Pharmacovigilance & Predictive Modeling for Protease Inhibitors  
**Program:** UDLAP Honors Program  
**Researcher:** Lissel Erandi Flores Polvo  

---

## 📌 Executive Summary & Focus
During Weeks 36 and 37, the project made significant technical strides by implementing Stanford NLP (Stanza) for clinical text processing, exploring ensemble machine learning methods with mentors, and establishing a structured strategy for adverse drug reaction vocabulary collection to optimize future model training.

---

## 🔬 Key Stages & Technical Development

### 1. Clinical NLP with Stanza & Stanford NLP (Week 36)
* **Tool Implementation:** Began working with Stanford NLP technologies, specifically leveraging **Stanza** to process and understand clinical narratives.
* **Named Entity Recognition (NER):** Utilized the clinical extension `mimic` alongside the `i2b2` NER model to classify and extract words and entities directly from patient narratives.

### 2. Random Forest & Dual-Mentor Progress Review (Week 37)
* **Advanced Machine Learning:** Participated in mentorship classes focusing on the **Random Forest** algorithm, analyzing ensemble learning methods and their application.
* **Dual-Mentor Evaluation Meeting:** Held a comprehensive progress review meeting with both research mentors. 
* **Strategic Directives:** Initiated structured work on extracting and collecting specialized vocabularies—focusing on both adverse drug reactions and clinical narrative content—to build robust features for advanced model training.

---

## 📈 Summary of Milestones (Weeks 36 – 37)
* **Text Mining Execution:** Successful integration of Stanza (`mimic` + `i2b2`) for clinical entity extraction.
* **Algorithmic Growth:** Theoretical and practical understanding of Random Forest models.
* **Strategic Alignment:** Direction established for large-scale vocabulary collection to feed the predictive modeling pipeline.
